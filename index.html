What you'll learn

Six weeks, shaped like a story.

Every good story climbs, peaks and lands. So does the course.

  1. Week 101

    The hook

    Open with a line people can't ignore, and drop the "so, basically".

    I almost missed the most important train of my life.

  2. Week 202

    People and places

    Describe characters and settings with simple, vivid words.

    I had ironed my shirt twice and printed my resume three times.

  3. Week 303

    Build it up

    Connect events with "just then", "suddenly" and "by the time".

    The train on platform 4 was going to Chennai. Ten minutes left.

  4. Week 404

    The big moment

    Pauses, pace and emphasis, so the best part lands.

    "Interview?" she asked. "Platform 1, beta. Run."

  5. Week 505

    Land the ending

    Finish with a line people repeat, then stop talking.

    The best introductions are not perfect. They are true.

  6. Week 606

    Story night

    Tell a two-minute story, live, to the whole cohort.

    Two minutes. One story. Twelve people listening.

See it in action

A 60-second story, unpacked

A story a student could tell after six weeks. Keep scrolling to see what makes it work.

Tuesday, 14 March
7:40 pm

the hookweek 1

I almost missed the most important train of my life.

people & placesweek 2

It was my first job interview in Bengaluru. I had ironed my shirt twice, printed my resume three times, and practised my introduction all night in front of the mirror.

build it upweek 3

At the station, the board said platform 4. I ran to platform 4. The train on platform 4 was going to Chennai. My heart dropped. Ten minutes left.

the big momentweek 4

Just then, an aunty selling coffee pointed at me and laughed. "Interview?" she asked. "Platform 1, beta. Run."

I ran faster than I have ever run in a shirt. I reached the interview four minutes late, completely sweaty, and the first question was, "So, tell us about yourself."

land the endingweek 5

So I told them this story. They laughed. I got the job.

Now I always say: the best introductions are not perfect. They are true.

This is for you if

  • you understand English well but freeze when it's your turn

  • you like learning with other people

Probably not for you if

  • you're starting from the very basics (1-on-1 classes are a better first step)
  • you want exam or IELTS score coaching
  • you'd rather watch videos than speak

A note from Reshma

    
Hi, I'm so glad you're here. I've met so many people who understand English perfectly and still go quiet the moment they have to speak it. Not because they don't know the words, but because nobody gave them a safe place to say them out loud.

    
    
That's what my classes are. A friendly, slightly silly, very real space to talk. We fix grammar as we go, we laugh at the tricky words, and week by week the pauses get shorter.

    
You don't need perfect English to start. You just need to start.
